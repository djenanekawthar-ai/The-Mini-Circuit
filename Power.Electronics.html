Power Electronics & Safety Measures.

While small Arduino or basic circuits typically operate at low, touch-safe voltage (3,3V or 5V), adding power electronics; like motors, relays, or external power supplies; introduces risks like short circuits, component damage, and potential fire hazards.

. Core Electrical Safety Measures

.Common grouding: Always connect the groung (GND) of your external power supply to the Arduino GND. Without a shared reference point, control signals will behave erratically and can cause unexpected voltage spikes.
.Separation of Power: Never power high-current devices directly from the Arduino's 5V or 3,3V pins. The Arduino's onboard regulator can only handle a few hundred milliamperes (mA) before overhearting or failing. Use an external power supply for the load and use the Arduino strictly for control.
.Current-Limiting Resistors: Always place a resistor in series with components like LEDs or transistor bases. Operating them Without a resistor creates a near-short-circuit condition that can burn out both the component and the Arduino's I/O pin.
